📖 Cheat sheet

Precision — of the fish you keep, how many are the one you wanted? (a clean bucket)

Recall — of all the right fish out there, how many did you land? (a full bucket)

Narrow & aimed = cleaner bucket, but you miss some. Cast wide = you get them all, plus junk. You usually can't have both.

🎣 The Angler's Dilemma

A tiny game about Precision vs. Recall

You're an angler. Each round, one kind of fish is the one you want. Rig your gear, then cast.

+ points for every right fish you keep  ·  − points for wrong fish you keep  ·  − points for right fish that get away

Your gear sets the shape of your catch box. The depth dial and the boat slide that box up/down and left/right over the water. Small + aimed = clean but misses a lot. Big = catches everything, junk included. There's no perfect rig — the best one depends on what each mistake costs.

    Every cast is a dot: precision across, recall up. Notice how the same angler had to sit in different corners of this plot depending on what each round's mistakes cost.

    • Fines for wrong fish → you drift right (high precision, narrow band).
    • Disaster for misses → you drift up (high recall, wide band).
    • Rare fish → accuracy lied; precision & recall told the truth.

    That's the whole lesson: a tool (or a model) isn't "good" or "bad" on its own. Every modeled decision has a tradeoff, and models are best used with explicit knowledge of that tradeoff.
